My strength app didn’t know about my cardio workouts. My cardio app didn't know about my diet. My diet app didn't know about any of my training. None of my fitness apps took into account my calendar or life outside the gym. What I needed was a single app that could put together the entire picture: my goals, my plan, my workouts, my diet, my recovery, and my schedule.

So I built it. It's called 95th:

{% include figure.html path=page.thumbnail_path caption=page.title url="https://95th.app/?ref=ybrikman-blog-introducing-95th" %}

It's a single app for iPhone and Apple Watch to get your cardio, strength, and body into the 95th percentile for your age group. I vibe coded it for myself because I was tired of juggling a dozen different apps. It worked out better than I ever expected, as a single app that has the full view of my health & fitness can do things no other app can. I now use 95th every single day, and it has been so useful to me, that I figured it might be useful to others too.

In this post, I'll cover four things that make 95th so unique and valuable, and then tell you how to get access:

## Set goals for life

In 95th, you set goals using percentiles, ranked against people of the same age and sex in the general population, with the 95th percentile as the default target (hence the name of the app). For example, here's what the 95th percentile looks like for a handful of exercises for a man 40-49 years old:

- **Run 5K**: 21 min
- **Bike 40K**: 1h 27min
- **Push-ups**: 43
- **Pull-ups**: 12

I find that the 95th percentile is the sweet spot: 

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/cost-benefit.png" %}

It's a high enough level of fitness to get you most of the health benefits, but not so high that it requires an unreasonable time investment or high risk of injury (as opposed to what competitive athletes go for, which is the 99th+ percentile). Moreover, as percentiles are defined by age group, percentile goals update automatically as you get older. For example, whereas the 95th percentile for a 5K in my 40s is 21 min, in my 50s, it'll be 23 min, and in my 60s, it'll be 25 min. So the 95th percentile is the right target for elite health whether you're 35 or 75.

## Plans that adapt to your life

Setting lofty goals is not enough; you also need a plan to achieve them. 95th has a number of proven plans built-in for strength, cardio, and diet, but the most powerful thing is that it _adapts those plans to fit your life_. 

First, it integrates with your calendar, so you can schedule your workouts just like you schedule meetings:

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/calendar.png" %}

Second, as things in your life change, 95th can adjust your plan accordingly. For example, if you have travel scheduled, 95th can reschedule your workout, and update it to accommodate the equipment you have available on the road. And if you get injured, you can track health issues in the app, and 95th will automatically update your plan to work around it:

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/health-issue-auto-update.png" %}

## Diet tracking without the busy work

95th not only helps you plan your exercise, but your diet too. Once again, a key focus is on adapting the diet to fit your life. You can import your favorite recipes, and plan your meals out on a calendar:

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/meals-calendar.png" %}

The pay-off for planning what you'll eat is that 95th can then automatically calculate portions for the people in your household, and turn your meal calendar into a grocery list:

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/groceries.png" %}

You can then automatically order those groceries on Instacart. Moreover, 95th will automatically add the meals in your calendar to your food diary. And if you eat an unplanned meal, you can add it to your diary by saying what you ate into your watch or taking a picture with your phone.

## A single pane of glass for health & fitness

95th has replaced more than a dozen apps for me: Strong for tracking strength workouts, Apple Workouts for tracking outdoor cardio workouts (running, biking), Wahoo and Urevo and ErgData for tracking indoor cardio workouts (indoor trainer, treadmill, rower), MyFitnessPal and Cronometer for tracking diet, and so on. By having all of this in a single app, 95th can do things no other app can.

For example, instead of using a spreadsheet, 95th can automate progressions for any exercise. You can define your current training max for squatting or running, use percentages in your workout plans (e.g., squat 3×5 @ 85% or run 400 m @ 85% pace), and specify rules for when the max should increase (e.g., +2.5 kg/workout for squat, or +10%/week for runs) or when it should pause or decrease (e.g., due to injury):

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/progressions.png" %}

Since 95th sees both your workout and diet plans, if you have a long cardio workout planned (>90 min), 95th can automatically review your diet, and give you fuel reminders throughout the day, and during the workout, including how much you should be ingesting in terms of carbs, salt, and water per hour:

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/fuel.png" %}

More generally, 95th reviews your plan and progress every week, and gives you feedback on what's progressing, what's stalled, what you can tweak in your diet, whether you're doing too much or not enough, and much more:

{% include image.html path="blog/95th/review.png" %}
