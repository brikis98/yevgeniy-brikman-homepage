In the third book of this series, Adrian, is still writing in his diary as he tries to survive a world overrun by the undead. The story expands his exposure to other survivors, both friendly and unfriendly, and begins to reveal the background behind the zombie outbreak. The parts dealing with other survivors are as enjoyable as in the previous books; the explanation for the zombie outbreak, however, is a big letdown.

<!--more-->

What I liked about the first two books was that they were gritty stories of survival: fighting zombies, finding food, securing shelter, encountering other survivors, dealing with injury and disease, and so on. Although zombies as a concept are a bit ridiculous, for the most part, this felt like a realistic tale of post-apocalyptic survival. And this 3rd book in the series continues most of that, with the stakes growing ever higher as Adrian comes across more and more survivors, who either end up relying on him, or trying to kill him. Most of this book is told from the perspective of Adrian's diary entries, mixed with a handful of tales from other perspectives of people surviving the apocalypse around the world.

However, this 3rd book also introduces a new element, which is an attempt to explain where the zombies came from. And frankly, the explanation is _awful_. I won't list any spoilers here, other than to say that it felt like a grossly cliched combination of the worst tropes, and it starts to move the books in a new direction that I worry will make them feel far less gritty and realistic. I think it would've been far better if they had just left it a mystery that we could never fully understand.

I'll try one more book in the series, but if it keeps moving in this direction, it might not be the right fit for me.
