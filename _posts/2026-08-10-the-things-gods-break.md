In the second book of this series, Lyra Keres finds herself trapped in Tartarus, the deepest pit of the underworld. As she works to find a way to escape, she realizes that she is not alone: the Titans have been trapped in Tartarus for centuries, and if Lyra finds a way out, they might be able to break free as well. While I enjoyed the first book in the series, this one felt like a pretty big let down: too much time travel, too much "that was my plan all along," and too much, for lack of a better term, porn.

<!--more-->

I liked 3 things about the first book:

1. The premise that the Greek gods are real and live with us in the modern world. 

2. The characters (humans, gods, demigods, monsters, and so on) were entertaining.

3. The 12 labors Lyra had to go through were well thought out, and kept the story moving along at a great pace.

This book mostly shifts focus to the Titans, and spends most of its time in Tartarus. Then the story introduces other gods, from outside Greek mythology, which dilutes the focus even more. And all throughout, there is time travel, which muddles things further. So much of (1) is gone.

While some of the characters from the first book are back in this one, including Lyra, who is great, a lot of the focus in this book is on the new characters—mostly the Titans—which is a bit jarring. That said, the Titans are reasonably well thought out characters themselves, so (2) still holds, for the most part.

As for the third item, the focus of this story is escaping from Tartarus, done mostly through a lot of weird time travel, and it just didn't work for me. It jumped back and forth, sometimes to scenes from the first book, sometimes to totally new scenes, and it just didn't pull me in nearly as much as item (3) ("I wonder what perverse labor they have lined up next??"). There is a big central mystery that pulls the story forward, but it didn't grab me, and the plot relies on a lot of deus ex machina to resolve major plot points, which is perhaps appropriate for a story about gods, but it doesn't make for fun reading.

Finally, as with the first book, this one has some seriously detailed and graphic sex scenes. It felt really out of place in a young adult novel of this sort, and instead of being hot or intriguing, these scenes just made me groan. Note that I listened to this book as an audiobook, so that just made it all the weirder.

There's still some great stuff in this series—the characters, the world building, the prose—but I'm not sure it's enough to merit picking up the next entry in this series.
