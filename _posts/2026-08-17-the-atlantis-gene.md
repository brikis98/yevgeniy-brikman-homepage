Dr. Kate Warner is a geneticist in Jakarta, Indonesia, working on a breakthrough treatment for autism. David Vale is a counterterrorism agent who has been tracking a shadowy organization called Immari International. When a mysterious structure is discovered buried in the ice off Antarctica, and two of Kate's young patients are abducted from her lab, the two of them are pulled into a conspiracy that reaches back to the origins of humanity itself. It's an intriguing premise, and it had potential, but the writing, plot, and especially the characters fail to deliver.

<!--more-->

The characters in particular are all 2-dimensional tropes. I guarantee that you can guess _exactly_ who they are based solely on my first paragraph. For Dr. Kate Warner, did you picture an attractive, brilliant, self-sacrificing woman who acts as the love interest and nurse? For David Vale, did you picture a handsome, tough, intensely competent, badass who wants to do the right thing, and gets lots of injuries and scars along the way? Groan. And believe me, the bad guys are even worse.

The plot mixes genetics, disease theory, ancient history, monks, Nazis, aliens, conspiracy theories, and a bunch of other elements. Focusing on a handful of these might have resulted in an intriguing story. But throwing all of them together and jumping back and forth across all of them makes things a bit too jumbled. There are a few interesting ideas in there, but unfortunately, they get overshadowed by all the other clutter.

And tying it all together is a writing style that feels a bit clunky and cliched. I think this was A.G. Riddle's first book, and I found his writing a bit smoother and more confident in later books (such as [The Long Winter series](/book-series/long-winter)), but this one was bumpy.

Overall, I struggled to get through this book, so I don't think I'll be continuing on to the rest of this series.
