Severian is a young apprentice in the Guild of Torturers, raised within the guild's ancient tower in the city of Nessus on a far-future world called Urth. When he commits the ultimate sin of his profession, showing mercy toward one of his victims, he is exiled from the guild and sent on a journey out of Nessus and into the strange world beyond. The idea of focusing on a guild of torturers seems like a unique premise for a story, but the book doesn't deliver on it at all, and instead focuses on a winding, random adventure that is not particular compelling.

<!--more-->

When I picked this book up, I thought we'd get into the mentality of a torturer. What kind of person would go into a job like that? How do they justify it to themselves? What does it do to their personality and their perception of the world? The book addresses none of these questions. Instead, Severian is kicked out of the torturers guild very early in the story, and the torturer focus is dropped almost entirely.

Instead, the book focuses on a strange fantasy tale with odd characters and weird magic. I didn't find any of it compelling. I didn't care about any of the characters. It wasn't clear where the plot was going or why I should keep reading. And now that I'm writing this review a couple months later, I can barely remember a single detail from the adventure part of this story. Not my cup of tea, and I will not be continuing this series.
