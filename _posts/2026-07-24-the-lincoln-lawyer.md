Mickey Haller is a criminal defense attorney who works out of the back seat of his Lincoln Town Car, driving between the courthouses of Los Angeles to defend bikers, con artists, drunk drivers, and drug dealers. When a Beverly Hills playboy is arrested for attacking a woman he picked up in a bar, he hires Haller to defend him, giving Mickey his first high-paying client in years, and what looks like it might be the easiest case of his career. I first met Haller in the [Harry Bosch series](/book-series/harry-bosch), found him to be a fun character, and figured I'd try out the series dedicated to him. I was not disappointed.

<!--more-->

I'm a fan of Michael Connelly's writing, and it's fun to see him take on a new character. In some ways, Haller is similar to Bosch; in some ways, he's very different. The other characters—especially the cartoonish bad guy—are not nearly as interesting. That said, the most interesting character, by far, is the city of LA itself (just as in the Bosch series). Here, we get to see the legal side of LA, which overlaps with the police department angle in the Bosch series, but has many of its own unique elements.

Haller is an entertaining character, and the story forces you to grapple with the question of how lawyers can stand to defend people they suspect, or know, are guilty. Everyone deserves a fair trial, but once you understand how fallible judges, juries, and lawyers are, it's hard to know what's truly fair.
