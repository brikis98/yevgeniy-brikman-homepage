In the fourth book of this series, Adrian and his adopted family at Auburn Lake work to expand their territory and deal with new arrivals. This part is as good as it was in the earlier books. Unfortunately, picking up on the themes introduced in book 3, this book introduces a lot more of a supernatural theme, and in my opinion, it's starting to go off the rails.

<!--more-->

I like zombie stories because they create an opportunity to see how humans would behave in a post apocalyptic world. The questions that interest me are how do people survive in a brutal reality where society has collapsed? How do you deal with danger being around every corner? What do you do for shelter? Where do you get food? What does an abandoned world look like? Can society be rebuilt? Does morality still matter? The zombies themselves are just a mechanism to force a story to look at these themes.

The early books of this series largely focused on exactly these items. The last book, and even more so this one, are becoming obsessed with the zombies themselves, and where they come from, and it's taking the story in a direction that is much less interesting to me. This book introduces a whole pile of, essentially, magic, and it feels like a significant departure from the semi-realistic, gritty survival focus the book had earlier. In fact, it invalidates most of the questions I find fascinating, replacing them with a focus on supernatural entities, rather than human themes.

As a result, I probably will not be continuing this series. So much potential wasted!
