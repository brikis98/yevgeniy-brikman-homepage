In Paris in 1895, Alfred Dreyfus, a young Jewish officer in the French army, is convicted of treason, stripped of his rank in front of a jeering crowd of twenty thousand, and sentenced to life imprisonment on Devil's Island. Watching his humiliation is Georges Picquart, the ambitious, newly promoted head of the counterespionage agency that "proved" Dreyfus passed secrets to the Germans, and who is convinced of his guilt. But not long after Dreyfus is shipped off to prison, Picquart stumbles on information suggesting that there is still a spy at large in the French military.
